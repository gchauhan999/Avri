# Image sources and licensing

Every photograph in `industries/`, `services/` and `projects/` is either a
**Pexels** image or a **CC0 / public-domain** image. Both allow commercial use,
modification and redistribution, and **none of them requires attribution** — so
nothing on those three sections needs a credit line.

The **product** photographs are different: 30 of them are CC BY or CC BY-SA,
which permit commercial use only if the photographer is named. They are
credited on the site's `/image-credits` page, linked from the footer, whose
data lives in `lib/image-credits.ts`. See
[`products/CREDITS.md`](products/CREDITS.md) for the full picture.

This file exists so the provenance of each picture can be checked later, and so
whoever replaces one knows what test it has to pass.

## The test any new image must pass

1. **You hold the rights** — your own photograph, a licence you bought, or a
   licence that permits commercial use (Pexels, CC0, CC BY with credit shown).
2. **No other company's branding is visible** — no logos on equipment, vehicles,
   hoardings or workwear. Two otherwise-good candidates were rejected here for
   exactly this: one showed a solar installer's branded vest, another an EV
   charger with the maker's name across it.
3. **Nothing implies work you did not do.** These are illustrative sector
   photographs, not records of Avri Energy projects. Replace them with your own
   site photography as it becomes available — that will always be more
   persuasive than stock.

For `industries/`, `services/` and `projects/`, attribution-required licences
were deliberately avoided — every slot had a good Pexels or CC0 option. The
product catalogue is where that stopped being possible: outside a handful of
categories there is simply no CC0 photograph of an RMU, a metering cubicle or a
bus duct, so CC BY / CC BY-SA images are used there and credited on
`/image-credits`.
